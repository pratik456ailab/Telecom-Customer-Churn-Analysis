# Telecom-Customer-Churn-Analysis
Telecom Customer Churn Analysis Using Exploratory data analysis (EDA)

Summary and Recommendations

Executive Summary: Telecom Customer Churn Analysis
This analysis investigates customer churn patterns in a telecom company, leveraging exploratory data analysis (EDA) to identify key drivers of customer retention and churn. The dataset includes 7,043 customers and 21 attributes spanning demographic details, service subscriptions, and billing information.


Key Insights from Visualizations:
1. Churn Rate Overview
•	26.54% of customers have churned, meaning 1 in 4 customers leave the service.
•	73.46% of customers are retained, indicating a need to address the factors influencing churn.

2. Demographic Influence on Churn
•	Senior Citizens are more likely to churn, with 41.6% of senior customers leaving, compared to only 23.6% of non-senior customers.
•	Customers with no dependents have a churn rate of 31%, while those with dependents churn at a lower rate of 15%.

3. Tenure and Churn Relationship
•	Customers with a tenure of 0–6 months have the highest churn rate (60%).
•	Long-term customers (tenure >24 months) have a much lower churn rate of 10.5%, showing that customer longevity is a strong retention factor.

4. Impact of Contract Type on Churn
•	Month-to-month contract customers experience a churn rate of 45%, while only 11% of yearly contract holders churn.
•	Customers with two-year contracts have the lowest churn (3.5%), suggesting that contract commitments significantly impact retention.

5. Service-Related Factors & Churn
•	Internet Service Type Matters:
o	Fiber optic users have a churn rate of 42%, while DSL users churn at 19%.
o	Customers without internet service churn only 7%, indicating that high-speed service dissatisfaction may be a factor.
•	Security & Tech Support Influence:
o	Customers with no online security churn at 46%, compared to 15% for those with security services.
o	Lack of tech support is linked to a 45% churn rate, whereas customers with support churn only 13%.

6. Billing and Payment Trends
•	Paperless Billing Customers Churn More:
o	E-billing customers churn at 33%, while those receiving paper bills churn at 19%.
•	Payment Method Impact:
o	Electronic check users churn at 45%, compared to 16% for automatic bank transfer users.
o	Credit card and mailed check users have the lowest churn (~14%), suggesting that frictionless payments encourage retention.

Conclusion & Recommendations:
1.	Encourage long-term contracts by offering discounts or perks for 1-year and 2-year commitments.
2.	Improve service quality for fiber optic customers, as they show a significantly higher churn rate.
3.	Bundle Online Security & Tech Support services into internet plans to retain high-risk customers.
4.	Revise billing strategies, as paperless billing and electronic checks correlate with higher churn.
5.	Implement loyalty programs for new customers to increase retention within the first 6 months, the most vulnerable period for churn.
This analysis successfully visualizes critical patterns in churn behavior, providing data-driven insights to improve customer retention strategies.
